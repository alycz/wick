Independent check at 21:58:00.493 UTC

Deribit BTC was $62,268.77 with 119.507 seconds left. I independently priced both calls with the normal distribution in Python and the archived lower and upper strike volatilities. The benchmark prices a sharp threshold using each strike’s own implied volatility held constant locally. It is a sensitivity check. The volatility smile and the distribution of returns over the last two minutes introduce uncertainty.

Wick takes the difference in theoretical call prices at K − 0.25% and K + 0.25%, then divides by the distance between the two strikes. At $62,250 this samples a $311.25-wide interval for a $65.27 one-standard-deviation move. At expiry the call spread pays progressively across that interval while the underlying contract pays at a single threshold. Wick also caps displayed marks at 1¢ and 99¢.

The Deribit snapshot at 21:57:44.189 UTC contained 55.63% mark IV at $62,000 and 52.37% at $62,500 on the nearest listed options, expiring November 19 at 08:00 UTC. Independently inverting their call bid and ask premiums produced IV ranges of 53.1–60.7% and 49.6–53.3%. Applying that overnight implied volatility to the remaining two minutes is an extrapolation. As a separate check, the prior hour of Deribit minute spot returns implied about a $49 two-minute move at the prevailing spot. Neither estimate establishes an exact probability.

Feed and timing defects

  • At 21:58:25.514 UTC a fallback to Derive moved the middle strike from 56.06¢ to 55.74¢. It returned to 56.09¢ on Deribit a second later. Both outside strikes held at 99¢ and 1¢ on those three ticks. Production pricing was subsequently restricted to Deribit.
  • The dated history had 55 older minute records tagged Derive across all expiries. All were reconstructed using only the spot and volatility from a preceding saved Deribit snapshot. For this expiry’s $62,500 strike at 15:02:00.465 UTC the corrected historical estimate is 89.72¢, versus the original 89.92¢. Originals are retained.
  • The production WebSocket sent additional model ticks for the expired event through 22:00:07.272 UTC. Those are post-expiry ticks, not settlement. The numerical comparisons here end before 22:00.

Gemini ultimately published $62,239.354. Only the $62,000 Yes share paid $1. One realized outcome cannot validate or invalidate forecast calibration.
